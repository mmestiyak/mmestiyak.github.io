What Kommercly Became, and What I Got Wrong on the Way

In November 2025 I wrote a post called Launch a WhatsApp-First Store in Minutes with Kommercly. The pitch was simple: copy a Google Sheet, paste the link, get a store that sends orders to your WhatsApp. Free for three months. No code. I believed every word of it.

A lot has changed since. Some of it I planned. Some of it I only understood after watching real sellers use the thing. This is the honest version.

What the first post promised

Let me restate it so you can hold me to it. The promise was speed. A working store in minutes, built from a sheet you already keep, with WhatsApp as the checkout. No agency, no monthly bill, no developer.

That promise is still the reason Kommercly exists. What changed is how you get there, and what you pay.

The Google Sheet moved from the front door to the side door

The sheet was the whole product in that first post. You copied a template, filled rows, pasted a link.

Then I watched sellers do it. Most did not have a tidy sheet. They had product photos in their phone gallery and a Facebook page. Asking for a Google Sheet first was asking them to do admin before they could sell.

So phone signup is now the main path. You sign up with a phone number, add products from your phone with a photo, a name, and a price, and you get a store at yourname.kommercly.com. The Google Sheet still works for sellers who already run their business in one, and those sellers keep it. New signups start on the phone.

Products stopped being one row

In the first version a product was a name, a price, and a photo. Real sellers kept hitting the same walls. So a product now supports:

  • Sizes and colours, up to five options on one product, each with its own price and its own photo.
  • A description with some formatting, written on a phone.
  • Categories you can rename, reorder, and hide.
  • Tags, used to show related products.
  • Stock that counts down.

None of that was in the November post. All of it is in the product now.

Orders became the daily habit

The old flow ended at WhatsApp. The order arrived, and after that you were on your own.

Now there is an Orders page. Every order arrives with the items, the delivery area, and the total. You mark it New, Confirmed, Shipped, or Delivered. The page shows a risk flag on an order so you can look closer before you ship. The server works out every total itself, so a tampered page cannot change what a customer owes.

On Premium you can book a Steadfast parcel from the order page instead of retyping the address into the courier dashboard. That is the part I am proudest of, because it is the part sellers asked for.

The store got a wardrobe

The first store had one look. I told myself that was focus. It was really a limitation.

Today there is a small gallery of looks: the two originals, Classic and Modern, plus six built for a type of shop (a bazaar, a gadget store, a homemade food page, and a few more). There is a design studio in the admin where you set the accent colour, the fonts, and the sections from your phone. A seller picks a look that matches their brand instead of living with mine.

I also wrote in a planning note that we would not build a second theme. I was wrong. Sellers judge a store in the first two seconds, and a clothing shop and a phone shop should not look the same.

A few more things that exist now

  • Your own domain, on Premium, instead of a kommercly.com subdomain.
  • A Bangla storefront, turned on per store, for sellers whose customers read Bangla.
  • Instagram and Facebook links in the store footer.
  • A sales analytics dashboard.
  • An accent colour pulled from your logo.

The pricing changed, and I owe you that one

The November post said “free 3-month trial, no card.” That is not what Kommercly does today.

Here is the current shape. The Free plan stays free, with up to 30 products, WhatsApp checkout, and 0% commission. New stores also get Premium free for 7 days. After that, Premium is ৳499 a month, ৳3,999 a year, or a ৳9,999 one-time Founding Lifetime price. New sellers can take a ৳99 intro first month. Billing is bKash by hand, because Stripe does not serve Bangladeshi merchants.

Why the change? A store builder costs money to run, and I would rather charge a small, clear price than quietly limit the free plan until it breaks. The free plan is real. It is just smaller than “three months of everything.”

I also removed testimonials I could not stand behind. If a quote is not from a real seller who said it, it does not belong on the page.

What I got wrong

Two things matter more than any feature.

The first is retention. Of the first 15 stores that ever took an order, 13 went quiet. I was busy adding sellers and not busy enough making them come back. Fixing activation came before chasing more traffic. A store that never takes a second order is not a business, for the seller or for me.

The second is building before asking. Some of the work in this post started as a guess. The Orders page, the risk flag, and Steadfast booking started as conversations with two sellers who were already using the product. The features that stuck came from sellers. The ones I invented mostly sat there.

What matters to me now

A few rules I keep coming back to:

  • Signup to a shared link in under five minutes, on a phone, 360 pixels wide.
  • A first WhatsApp order inside the seller’s first week.
  • Honest copy. If I cannot check a claim, I do not make it.
  • Organic only. No ads, no boosted posts. If the product cannot grow by word of mouth from real sellers, a bigger budget will not fix that.

What is next

Courier booking beyond Steadfast, starting with Pathao. A direct COD order form for sellers who want orders without WhatsApp. Deleting a store and changing your login email from the phone. A real case study from a seller who is actually selling, once I have one.

If you are a seller reading this

Start on the free plan. Add a few products from your phone. Share the link in your page bio and in your product posts. Take the first order on WhatsApp. If it helps, keep going. If it does not, tell me why, because that is how the last version got better.

I am one developer, and I still hate unnecessary complexity. That part of the November post holds up.
